How to Use This Field Guide

Before You Begin

This is not a policy manual. It is a field guide — a compact, illustrated reference built to help you find your way.

Like any field guide, it rewards a little orientation up front. Spend two minutes here and the rest of the handbook will make more sense.

How It Is Organized

The handbook is built around seven chapters, arranged in a deliberate sequence:

  1. Getting Your Bearings — the first things to understand when you arrive
  2. The Lay of the Land — a visual map of how the organization is structured
  3. Field Notes for the First Week — a day-by-day companion for your first days
  4. Tools of the Trade — what you will need and how to set it up
  5. Ways of the Work — how we communicate, decide, and collaborate
  6. Field Marks & Characters — how to recognize the culture and the roles you will encounter
  7. Signs & Signals — a key to the symbols used throughout, references for going deeper, and a note on how this guide was made

You need not read the chapters in order. Start where you need to. Return when you want to. The sequence is a suggestion, not a requirement.

How to Navigate

Page by page. At the bottom of every page, you will find links to the previous and next pages. The format tells you what you are turning to: “← p. 2 — How to Use This Guide” means the previous page is a spread about navigating the handbook, starting at page 2. Use the left and right arrow keys on your keyboard to move between pages, or press Escape to return to the Contents.

By the Contents. The table of contents lists every chapter with its starting page number. It is the fastest way to jump to a specific topic. You can reach it from any page — look for the “Contents” link at the bottom.

By cross-reference. Throughout the handbook, marginal notes point you to related content elsewhere. These are handwritten-style annotations in the margin. When one says “↪ See page 15,” it is a real person telling you where to go next.

By page number. Every page carries its number at the bottom outer corner. Front matter uses roman numerals (i, ii). The main body uses arabic numerals (1, 2, 3…). A page number is a stable address — if someone tells you “the decision process is on page 17,” you can find it.

How to Read the Symbols

The handbook uses several kinds of visual markers. Once you recognize them, they become a second language.

Stamps mark official items — the edition, a chapter closing, a certification. They appear in faded navy, as if pressed with an ink stamp. They are set slightly askew, like a real stamp applied by hand.

Official

Marginal notes are asides written in the margin in an informal hand. They are commentary from someone who has been here before you — a tip, a warning, a pointer to something useful. They are not required reading, but they often reward attention. Marginal notes appear in a terracotta color, as if penciled in the margin of a field guide.

Callouts are boxed notes that interrupt the main flow. They carry information that deserves a pause.

This is a callout. It marks something important enough to set apart from the surrounding text. A callout says: pay attention to this one.

Rules and ornaments divide sections. A thin hairline rule marks a boundary. A centered ornament marks a pause or a transition.

Diagrams explain structure visually. Some diagrams are interactive — you can click on a territory or a node to reveal more detail. These moments are optional. Everything a diagram shows is also described in the surrounding text.

Page numbers in the front matter use roman numerals (i, ii). Page numbers in the body use arabic numerals (1, 2, 3…). A spread (two facing pages) carries the right-hand page number.

A Note on Voice

This handbook was written by a person, not a committee. It has opinions. It has a tone. It tries to be useful before it tries to be comprehensive.

The marginal notes are written as if someone who has worked here marked up their own copy and handed it to you. They are not always neutral. They are always honest.

If something in this handbook is wrong, outdated, or unclear, that is a bug. Write it down. Fix it. Hand it to the next person better than you found it.

This field guide is never finished. Annotate yours. Add what you learn. Pass it on.
